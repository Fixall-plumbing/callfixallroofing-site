Roof Replacement

What you get.

Down to the wood

We tear off every layer. It's the only way to find soft or rotted decking and replace it before the new shingles go on. New decking is an add-on in the price table, because nobody knows how much until the old roof is off.

The stuff under the shingles

Ice-and-water shield, underlayment and drip edge. Nobody sees it from the street. It's what keeps the water out when the wind drives rain sideways.

New flashing, not reused

Old metal is where a lot of leaks start, so it doesn't go back on. Chimney flashing is its own add-on in the price table.

An attic that breathes

Ridge venting lets hot, wet air out of the attic. A stuffy attic cooks shingles from underneath and feeds mold. A ridge vent and extra attic ventilation are an add-on in the price table, and the inspection tells you if your attic needs it.

Cleanup, then photos

Magnet sweep for nails, a full cleanup, and photos of the finished roof and the layers under it. You see what you paid for.

What’s included

  • Full tear-off down to the decking
  • A look at the decking once the old roof is off
  • Ice-and-water shield, underlayment and drip edge
  • New flashing where the roof meets walls and pipes
  • Magnet sweep for nails and a full cleanup
  • Photos of the finished roof and the layers under it

Roof Replacement FAQ

Roof replacement questions.

Straight answers, no runaround.

Get a quote

How much does a roof replacement cost in Monmouth County?

Our ballpark ranges by home size and shingle are right on this page. They're for budgeting, not a quote. Your real price depends on the pitch, how many layers come off and what the decking looks like, and you get it in writing after the free inspection.

How do I know if I need a new roof or just a repair?

Curling, cracking or missing shingles across the whole roof, granules piling up in the gutters, or more than 1 leak usually means replacement. One bad spot usually means a repair. We show you the photos and tell you which. If it's a repair, we sell you a repair.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in NJ?

Usually not. Replacing the roof covering on a detached one- or two-family home is ordinary maintenance under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7, so there's no construction permit. If your house sits in a local historic district, the town may want its own approval first.

Can you put a new roof over the old one?

We tear off. The building code caps how many layers a roof can carry, and nobody can see or fix rotted decking through old shingles. A tear-off is the only way to know what's under there.

How long does a roof replacement take?

A typical single-family reroof is a 1 to 3 day job, weather allowing. More layers, a steep pitch or a lot of bad decking add time. We give you the schedule when we quote the job.

What shingles do you use?

The 3 shingle options and their ballpark ranges are in the price table on this page. We walk you through the difference when we quote your roof.

Do you offer a warranty?

Ask about warranty options on materials and workmanship when we quote your job. Whatever we offer, you get it in writing with the bid.
